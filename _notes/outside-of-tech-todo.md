the person links to the way you work, without becoming a life story.

## 1. Gather material (you)
- [ ] 3–6 photos: painting or pottery you've made, an ultra race, scuba or a New Zealand trip, a workshop or games night. Landscape works best; rough is fine.
- [ ] Pick one or two "proudest" things: a specific ultra (distance or name), a piece you made, a book you're writing (working title or genre?).

- [ ] Which volunteering mattered most to you, and why, in one sentence each.

## 2. Decide the angle
- [ ] Choose 3–4 themes, e.g. **Making things** (paint, pottery, knit, write), **Endurance** (ultra-running, swimming, diving), **Bringing people together** (workshops, games nights, networks), **Giving back** (volunteering).
- [ ] For each theme, write one line on how it shows up in your work, e.g. "Ultras taught me to pace a long project", "Pottery: you can't rush the kiln".
- [ ] Keep the tone light: "the fun bit" stays.

## 3. Content to write
- [ ] A short opener (1–2 sentences) on who you are away from the screen.
- [ ] One card per theme: a title, 1–2 sentences, and a photo or small sketch.
- [ ] A "currently" line that's easy to update: reading, making, training for. Keep it vague enough that it doesn't go stale quickly.
- [ ] Optional: a "fun facts" strip (3 short items), e.g. the turtle sanctuary, the competitive games nights.

## 4. Design (ask Claude once the material is ready)
- [ ] Turn the paragraphs into a photo or sketch grid in the whiteboard style, like the skills cards.
- [ ] Compress images to under 300 KB and add short alt text for each.
- [ ] Check it on phone width.

## 5. Keep private
- [ ] No identifiable people in photos without their OK, no exact locations or home area, and nothing about working rights or visas.
